Ge'ez & the model

Why Ge'ez, and how the model works

Thesis chapter 1 argues that Ge'ez has five properties that make it suitable for computation. Chapter 3 builds the cipher model on top of them.

Five properties Pictographic Ideographic Astrographic Numeric Syllographic The cipher model References

Five properties of the script

Ge'ez is treated as more than a set of sounds. The thesis combines five properties into one framework.

በ

Pictographic

Shapes drawn from nature and daily life, stylized into proto-graphs and then classical fidels.

ረ

Ideographic

Consonant classes carry social and moral ideas, e.g. Re'es for leadership and Bèt for shelter.

ፀ

Astrographic

26 classes × 7 orders = 182 syllographs, matched to the 182 days of one equinox.

፲

Numeric

Each root carries a base value. Across seven orders the values span 1 to 5,600.

ሀ

Syllographic

Every glyph joins a consonant to a vowel (an abugida), with homophone letters that keep distinct meanings.

Pictographic property

Following Scelta (2001) and Bekerie (1997), each graph began as an abstract picture of a living thing, a tool or a sky feature.

Syllographic property and ሞክሼ ፊደላት

Ge'ez is an abugida: each glyph carries its vowel. Mokshe fidelat are letters that sound alike in modern speech but carry different meanings.

"H" letters ሀ ሐ ኀ

  • ሀ after ሃሌ (praise); existence, as in ሀለወ.
  • ሐ after ሐመር (ship); the sea and navigation.
  • ኀ after ብዙኃን (the many) or ኀርም (net).
  • ኀለየ to praise vs. ሐለየ to think.

"S" letters ሰ ሠ

  • ሰ after እሳት (fire); heat and craft.
  • ሠ after ንጉሥ (king); dawn and rule.
  • ሰረቀ to climb vs. ሠረቀ to steal.

"A" letters አ ዐ

  • አ after አሌፍ (alpha); origin.
  • ዐ after ዓይን (eye); vision and springs.
  • አመተ female servant vs. ዐመተ year.

"Ts" letters ጸ ፀ

  • ጸ after ጸሎት (prayer).
  • ፀ after ፀሐይ (sun).
  • ፈጸመ to complete vs. ፈፀመ to strip bare.

The cipher model

The thesis describes the cipher as a deterministic finite automaton over the binary alphabet {0, 1}. At each recursion trₖ, k = 1 … 8:

V[i]   = W[i] · X[i] + B[i]
A[i]   = 1  if V[i] > 0,  else 0
X[k+1] = A[k]                              feedback
C      = A(tr1) ‖ A(tr2) ‖ … ‖ A(tr8)      64-bit block

Each character is an 8×8 grid. Columns 1–2 and row 8 are flag bits, set to 0. The remaining 42 bits hold the character's shape.

The thesis proposes the 64-bit weight matrix W and 64-bit bias matrix B as the key, for 128 bits per character, and suggests extending it to 512 bits by adding hidden layers.
